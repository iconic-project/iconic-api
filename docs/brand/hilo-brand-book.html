05 · Voice & Tone

How we sound.

Our voice is constant. Our tone shifts. Six principles below — they govern every word we publish, send, or paste into a slide.

P1

Outcome-first.

Lead with the number, not the activity. "Recovered $84,400 last month" beats "we ran a campaign".

P2

Plain over polished.

If a sentence sounds like marketing, rewrite it. We use the words operators use.

P3

Specific beats clever.

Property names, channels, dollar figures, dates. Concrete details earn trust faster than adjectives.

P4

Respect the brand we serve.

The property is the protagonist. We're the system underneath. Never compete with their voice.

P5

Short sentences. Earned long ones.

Default to clarity. Lengthen only when nuance demands it.

P6

One feeling per sentence.

Don't pile on. A confident statement loses its weight when it's chasing a second adjective.

Use
  • "Recovered $84,400 from abandoned bookings last month."
  • "OTA dependency is the largest cost line you can move."
  • "We charge a flat $15,000/month. Twelve-month contract. Full-service execution."
  • "Your guest experience is the asset. We translate it into revenue."
  • "Net revenue generated. That's the number."
Lose
  • "Unlock the power of next-gen revenue intelligence."
  • "World-class, best-in-class, cutting-edge."
  • "Synergy, alignment, holistic, ecosystem."
  • "Are you struggling with low direct bookings?"
  • "Click here to learn more."

Color story

Three voices. Cobalt for action. Ink for authority. Paper for rest. Anything else is a guest, not a host.

HILO Cobalt

The voice of action. Reserved for primary CTAs, focus states, brand moments, and the wordmark in feature contexts. Used sparingly so it always lands.

Typography

Satoshi Variable for everything — wordmark, display moments, body, tabular figures, and code. One family, hierarchy carried by size and case rather than aggressive weight stepping. Most copy lives at 400 Regular; 700 Bold is reserved for the wordmark and inline emphasis; 500 Medium for small uppercase CTAs; 300 Light for fine print only. The full type scale is documented in the design system.

Photography

We work in hospitality photography — moody, considered, hour-of-day specific. The property is the subject; we are the frame around it. Four registers, never people-as-models, and a single hour of light per asset.

Subject categories

Architecture · golden hour

Architecture

Quiet hero shots — façade, courtyard, empty pool at the golden hour or blue hour. No people, no occupation. The room before service. The lobby before guests. Symmetry, depth, a single point of light.

Detail · texture

Detail

Close crops — a turned-down bed, a wax-sealed envelope, a folded napkin, a bath drawn in marble, brass tap, candle wick caught mid-light. Texture over scale. Tactile, not styled-flat.

Environment · place

Environment

The sense of place — the city at first light, a coastline aerial, a jungle canopy, a desert dune at dusk. Establishes geography. The property's host context, never the property itself.

Guest · abstracted

Guest, abstracted

A hand on a banister. The hem of a robe. A foot on warm stone. Movement, gesture, the edge of the frame. Never a face. The guest is implied, never modeled. If you can identify the person, recompose.

Color grading direction

G1

Pull highlights down

No blown skies, no glare on chrome. Cap whites at 92% to retain texture.

G2

Lift shadows lightly

Open the blacks just enough to read the room. Never crush — the depth is the drama.

G3

Warm whites

White balance leans tungsten. No cool fluorescent cast. Walls should read off-cream.

G4

Slight desaturation

Pull saturation 10–15%. Color should feel observed, not amplified.

G5

Single hour of day

Match every photo in a set to one light condition. Mixed times read as a stock library.

G6

Grain over noise

If grain is needed, add it intentionally. Suppress digital noise — film texture, never sensor texture.

Composition rules

C1

Negative space wins

40% of every frame is air. We need it for type — and luxury reads as restraint.

C2

One subject per frame

If there are two stories, take two photos. Density signals discount.

C3

Symmetry or strong asymmetry

Center the bed, or push it hard left. Never split the difference.

C4

Eye line at hospitality height

Camera at 1.4–1.6m for room shots. Standing-tourist height looks transactional.

C5

Aspect ratios

4:5 (vertical) for social. 3:2 for editorial and proposals. Never 16:9 in feed — too cinematic for photography.

C6

No watermark on partner imagery

The HILO mark goes on our originals only. Partner photography is theirs, attributed.

Copy-on-photography treatment rules

T1

Scrim, always

A 0–55% black gradient bottom-up, or radial for centered quotes. Never type on raw photography — readability over poetry.

T2

Near-white, not pure white

Type at rgba(255,255,255,0.95). Pure white reads as tech. The slight softness reads as editorial.

T3

Satoshi Bold, uppercase

700 Bold + UPPERCASE for type on photography. Lighter weights disappear at small sizes. Tracking normal (0).

T4

Maximum 7 words

If the line breaks past two rows on a 4:5 frame, cut it. The point is to land — not to read aloud.

T5

Anchor to one corner

Bottom-left for stories, bottom-right for the wordmark, dead-center for principle quotes. Never floating mid-frame.

T6

Sentence case · period at end

"Net revenue generated." reads like a statement. "Net Revenue Generated" reads like a feature name.

T7

No type on detail crops

The texture is the message. A caption underneath the image is fine; type on top compromises both.

T8

HILO mark at 14pt, optical bottom-right

Optical alignment, not pixel-perfect. 12px from bottom edge, 14px from right edge. Same on every post.

Format rules — both platforms

F1

Open with the number

Or the property name. Or the place. Never with "Excited to share" or "Big news".

F2

One image per post

No carousels of stats, no template-driven slide decks. The single image is the discipline.

F3

Hashtags at end · maximum 3

Inline mentions of properties only with permission and tagged. Never hashtag the property name.

F4

Sentence-case captions

Title Case in social copy reads as marketing-trying-too-hard. Sentence case reads as a person speaking.

F5

No emoji in copy

Anywhere. The wordmark and the type carry the brand — emoji compete with both. (Exception: a single ◆ as a section break in long captions, sparingly.)

F6

Properties named only with permission

Default is anonymous. "A property in Madrid" beats name-dropping without sign-off — every time.

10 · Guardrails

What never ships in our name.

When in doubt, these win — over taste, over speed, over the urge to sound like everyone else in tech.

Don't compete with the property's voice.

The hotel is the protagonist of the guest's experience. We are the system underneath. Our copy never appears in front of guests except in the property's voice.

Don't promise outcomes we can't measure.

If we can't tie it to net revenue generated, we don't claim it. "Brand awareness", "engagement", "thought leadership" — out.

Don't write about technology for its own sake.

AI and automation are tools, not features. Lead with what they do for the property, never with what they are.

Don't use stock people in imagery.

Ever. Faces are a slippery slope to a category we are not — generic SaaS. The guest is implied, not modeled.

Don't open a sentence with a question to the prospect.

"Are you struggling with..." is a tell. We open with a number, a place, or a position.

Don't use the word "solution".

It's a tax word. We say "system", "approach", "lever", or — better — what the thing actually does.

Don't ship a deck without a number.

Every HILO presentation contains at least one specific dollar figure or percentage point in the first three slides. If the answer is "we don't have one yet", we work the deal harder.
